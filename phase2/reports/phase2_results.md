"Silver" = agreement with the unanimous WorldCover/Esri 2020–21 consensus (easy cells, optimistic); "independent" = GHSL / WSF-Evolution / JRC products never used for training or model selection; ground-truth accuracy needs the gold sample, which is **PENDING_INTERPRETATION**._

## Key findings (evidence in the sections below)

1. **Within-landscape agreement is high, transfer is not.** Best baseline (xgb, landsat_terrain) reaches test macro-F1 0.979 vs silver on 3 km spatial blocks, but leave-one-window-out macro-F1 is 0.57–0.82: block CV inside the training landscapes over-states transfer to an unseen landscape. Errors concentrate on agriculture vs natural vegetation.
2. **A classifier trained on the 2020–21 label epoch does not transfer to the Landsat-5 era.** It maps 75 % of the corridor as built-up in 1990 (GHSL built-surface fraction 7 %; WSF settled share in §F). Cells that independent products call stable are browner and brighter in early years; candidate explanations (not separated here): sensor/processing differences, real greening, rainfall regime. The pattern is consistent with 'low greenness' being read as built-up.
3. **Remedies:** multi-year stable-segment labels (R1) reduce the early-year bias (validation MAE built vs WSF 0.260 → 0.209); pseudo-invariant normalisation alone (R2) makes it worse (0.418). Selected by the pre-declared rule: **R1R2**. Residual bias remains → historical maps are reported only inside the area of applicability (§F).
4. **The area of applicability does not flag the early-year error** (97 % of the corridor is inside the AOA in 1990): the residual error is therefore not explained by feature-space extrapolation (consistent with class overlap inside the training domain). An AOA-gated HMM still lowers the test-block error of built share vs WSF in every window (khadakwasla_mutha 0.213 → 0.143, baramati_agri 0.555 → 0.489, mulshi_ghats 0.041 → 0.011), but Baramati remains strongly biased: pre-2015 built-up maps are NOT defensible there.
5. **False built-up tracks drought years in the corridor and Baramati** (Mulshi weaker, p≈0.06; consistent with dry-season fallow being read as built-up). On land that WSF/GHSL/JRC independently show as never settled, the 2021 model's false built-up rate falls as the previous monsoon's rainfall rises (khadakwasla_mutha ρ=-0.44 (p=0.007), baramati_agri ρ=-0.37 (p=0.027), mulshi_ghats ρ=-0.32 (p=0.059)); in the AOA-gated HMM product of the selected variant (R1R2) it weakens (khadakwasla_mutha ρ=-0.06, baramati_agri ρ=-0.31, mulshi_ghats ρ=-0.15). Interannual rainfall variability is associated with land-cover map error and must be treated as a covariate of map uncertainty.
6. **Urban expansion is quantified from WSF, not from our maps:** our built-up series falls after 2005 (artefact), WSF grows monotonically (6863 → 17212 ha in the corridor, 1990→2015) and shifts to infill (0.75 of new area in 2010–2015). Conversion is associated with proximity to existing built-up and population density in both sources (spatial-CV AUC khadakwasla_mutha · ours 0.807, khadakwasla_mutha · wsf 0.89, baramati_agri · ours 0.889, baramati_agri · wsf 0.968).
7. **Annual 30 m index series time urbanisation poorly:** the highest-recall change-point method (pelt_mean, post_ndvi) recovers the WSF year within ±2 y for 39% of independently urbanised cells, while flagging a break of |Δ| ≥ 0.1 on 87% of stable open land (crop dynamics, sensor steps).
8. **Coupled supply–response:** monsoon rainfall is associated with the NEXT dry season's green-cropland share in every window (Spearman on first differences: khadakwasla_mutha ρ=+0.50 (p=0.002, n=35), baramati_agri ρ=+0.62 (p=0.000, n=35), mulshi_ghats ρ=+0.34 (p=0.046, n=35)), while local reservoir surface area is not — an association, not a causal claim.
9. **Several requested products cannot be made honestly with the data available** (flood-event maps, crop types, 10 m river width, district-wide 30 m maps, ground-truth accuracy) — listed as PENDING with the reason, not filled in.
